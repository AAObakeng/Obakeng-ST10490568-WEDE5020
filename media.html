INTERVIEWS


1. COMPLEX - CARTUNEZ VS XPERIMENT

After the release of xperiment, Ken Carson spoke with Dimas Sanfiorenzo for Complex about his next project, Cartunez.

Ken explained that he felt he had moved away from his natural sound while making xperiment and wanted to approach Cartunez differently.

Ken Carson: "I feel like on Xperiment, I was shying away from my natural sound. On Cartunez, I'm not really doing that at all."



2. THE FADER - POP-UP AND THE XPERIMENT ERA

During the LES pop-up in July, The FADER covered Ken Carson outside ESSX as fans gathered around the event.

The event became crowded as fans reacted to Carson and the merchandise, with security eventually becoming overwhelmed. Inside the pop-up, Carson spent around ten minutes watching FIFA and interacting with fans before being brought back inside.

The event demonstrated the chaotic and highly interactive energy surrounding the xperiment era.



3. HYPEBEAST - STYLE AND CREATIVE DIRECTION

During his Maximum Volume digital cover interview, Ken Carson discussed his personal style, Opium's fashion identity and his approach to creativity.

On style: "I feel like each of us has our own island of Opium, especially when it comes to fashion. I think I keep it more basic in comparison to the other Opium rappers. But I'm also always on my rockstar trash shit."

On becoming a creative director: "I'd love to step into a designer role someday. I feel like I'm already on my Creative Director shit because I creative direct all of my own stuff."

On evolving: "I think my style evolves every single day. A lot of people like to write stuff off really quickly, and I make sure to never write anything off because I feel like anything can grow on me."

PRESS COVERAGE

The rollout of xperiment received coverage from music, entertainment and industry publications. The coverage focused on the album announcement, live previews, visual identity and promotional campaign.


THE FADER - JUNE 19, 2026

The FADER reported on the announcement of xperiment and its planned July 3, 2026 release.

The coverage also highlighted Ken Carson teasing new material during Summer Smash with Playboi Carti and previewing a 2hollis track during his Rolling Loud performance alongside Playboi Carti, Destroy Lonely, Young Thug and Lil Tecca.


UNIVERSAL MUSIC CANADA / THAT ERIC ALPER

Universal Music Canada and That Eric Alper framed xperiment as the follow-up to More Chaos, which debuted at number one on the Billboard 200.

The coverage highlighted the futuristic science-fiction short films directed by Joy Divizn, the USB-in-the-neck album artwork and the three-day London pop-up at Lab Store Westend.


BILLBOARD - ROLLING LOUD

Billboard's coverage of Ken Carson's Rolling Loud performance described the headlining set as a performance that felt bigger than a standard festival appearance.

The performance became a cornerstone of the xperiment rollout. Rather than drop singles online, Carson turned the stage into his first testing ground. During headlining sets at Rolling Loud Orlando in May and Roskilde Festival in Denmark on July 2, he debuted unreleased xperiment cuts like “Out Of My Body” and “Where Do I Start” live, months before the album's July 3 release. The shows weren't just promotion — they were part of the narrative. By previewing the material in front of massive crowds, Carson built hype organically and let fans experience the new sound in its intended environment: loud, chaotic, and communal. It set the tone for xperiment as a project built for performance first, streaming second. By turning festivals and pop-ups into live listening sessions, he created urgency that no playlist placement could match. Clips of the unreleased songs immediately flooded TikTok, Twitter, and fan pages, with “Out Of My Body” and “Cover My Ears” with Playboi Carti becoming early standouts before they were even on streaming. The approach also reinforced the era's identity. xperiment was marketed as a sensory, tour-driven project, and the performances made that feel real. Instead of hearing a polished studio version first, fans got the raw, mosh-pit version — complete with new stage lighting, distorted visuals, and Carson testing vocal deliveries live. That strategy paid off. By the time xperiment dropped on July 3, 2026, the audience already knew the choruses. The album debuted at No.7 on the Billboard 200, and the rollout was credited for building one of the most engaged fan conversations of the year. For Carson, the message was clear: with xperiment, the show came before the stream.

CRITIC REVIEWS

xperiment received mixed reactions from music critics. Reviews discussed whether the album successfully delivered on its experimental concept while also examining Carson's production, vocals, features and songwriting.


PITCHFORK - 5.0/10

Reviewer: Eli Enis

Pitchfork gave xperiment a score of 5.0 out of 10. The review took a mixed-to-negative position on the project and questioned whether Carson moved far enough away from the sound associated with his previous work.

The review argued that Carson sounded creatively exhausted with the rage formula and suggested that the album's lyrics, production and guest appearances remained connected to the same formula that the project attempted to move away from.

PRAISE

The review highlighted "Deaf Note" featuring Playboi Carti and "Possession" as examples of Carson's personality coming through particularly strongly.

"Ghost" featuring Lil Uzi Vert and "Drug Kit" featuring Young Thug were identified as some of the strongest tracks on the album.

CRITICISM

The review was particularly critical of "ShadesOn" featuring 2hollis, describing it as the weakest track and arguing that the performances did not adapt enough to the production.




SLANT MAGAZINE

Slant Magazine also questioned how much xperiment actually deviated from the sound of A Great Chaos and More Chaos.

The review praised Carson's vocal style, including his snickers, vocal changes and impulsive delivery, but argued that these qualities remained the main area in which the artist consistently succeeded.

Slant Magazine also identified "ShadesOn" as one of the album's weakest tracks.



ALLMUSIC

AllMusic gave a more positive assessment of xperiment. The review stated that Carson maintained his post-rage rap sound while introducing more variation in beats, flows and his overall approach than on previous releases.



BILLBOARD - KYLE DENIS

Kyle Denis of Billboard gave the album a favourable review. The coverage described Carson as a true niche superstar and highlighted his ability to connect with his fanbase while still introducing surprises throughout the project.



OTHER REACTIONS

Legends Will Never Die gave xperiment a rating of 3.5 out of 5. The review suggested that the album was not as experimental as expected, while also describing its rage and trap production as less watered down than More Chaos.

Fan reactions were divided. Some listeners described the album as being among Carson's best work, while others felt that the project's experimental concept was not reflected strongly enough in the music.
